# First pilot: Density and vehicle alerts on a normal road

Start with one fixed-camera view of a straight road segment with known permitted travel directions. Use pilot camera as the first candidate. Inspect the original view before drawing zones. One clip should exercise the existing wrong-way rule and a longer recording should show ordinary traffic for comparison. Treat the intersection as a later pilot with its own geometry and labels.

## Task checklist

- [ ] **1. Verify the pilot camera inputs (A + B).** Inspect pilot_camera.mp4, the handoff demo clip and the saved frame-level tracking log in data/tuan4/data/tracking_log_full.csv. Confirm frame numbers and elapsed times align with the source and document any tracker resets. Use the demo timestamps to locate positive examples in the full recording. Reserve contiguous sections for evaluation and keep the embedded demo interval out of the test set if it is used to tune. **Done:** source map and split list exist. If only one recording is available, describe results as a within-recording pilot.
- [ ] **2. Draw the road profile (A).** Mark one visible road polygon per evaluated direction and a short, straight speed measurement section with two transverse gates. Record which movements are permitted, where turning vehicles should be excluded, and which areas are obscured. Check the profile against the source resolution. Measure ground areas and the gate separation where reliable references exist. **Done:** an overlay on several frames shows correct boundaries, directions and gates.
- [ ] **3. Measure density first (B).** Reuse frame-level boxes from the saved log, then check the same calculation on output from the existing YOLOv8s and ByteTrack pipeline. Use each box's bottom center to assign one zone per vehicle at each sample. Start with 1 Hz samples and 10-second mean counts. Report total and class counts, vehicles present in each zone, and vehicles per 100 m² if area is known. Preserve observed zero separately from unavailable observation. **Done:** a replay chart and CSV agree, and independent frame counts give MAE and signed bias per zone, including crowded frames.
- [ ] **4. Reproduce and evaluate wrong-way alerts (A).** Run the shipped rule on its calibrated pilot camera view first. On a scored road section, require persistent motion against the permitted direction after minimum displacement. Keep separate coverage notes for directions where the rule is disabled or unreliable. Review legal turns and track jumps, and log one candidate per episode with track ID, time and evidence. **Done:** a full replay is compared with labeled events and normal sections. Report event precision, recall, false alerts per hour and detection delay where the labels support them.
- [ ] **5. Score unusual speeds (A).** Estimate one speed per vehicle using elapsed time between gates and measured distance along an approximately straight path. Score at the exit gate against prior distinct vehicles from the same section and direction. Compare rolling mean and standard deviation with rolling median and median absolute deviation on the same vehicles. Require enough peers and a minimum speed difference. Hold scoring when timing, tracking or the peer sample is unreliable. If distance is unavailable, score unusually short traversal times in seconds and label them as a relative pace proxy. **Done:** manually time reference traversals, report measurement error, and review fast flags and missed cases against independently scored samples. Keep the peer average internal.
- [ ] **6. Verify one combined replay (A + B).** Run a single tracker pass to produce a zone density series and both candidate alert types. Keep source timestamps and overlay the flagged tracks for review. Record throughput and alert delay separately. **Done:** save the road profile, replay command, density CSV, event log, evaluation results and evidence clips. Record separately any metric that lacks enough labeled examples.

## Dependencies

Tasks 1 and 2 support density measurement and both alert rules. After the first density chart, tasks 4 and 5 can proceed independently. Finish with the combined replay. Suggested sampling settings are pilot defaults. Tune thresholds on the development portion and freeze them before evaluation. Forecasting is a later milestone after density measurement is validated.

## pilot camera tuning decision

Use the short demo and its archived track log for development. Before any full pilot camera evaluation, exclude the overlapping footage around 13:47:00 through 13:48:02, verify the exact cut against the source, and reinitialize trajectory state for each evaluation segment.

A replay of the shipped wrong-way logic on the archived demo boxes tested starting angles of 165, 162, 160 and 155 degrees against persistence of 0.6, 0.8 and 1.0 seconds. Other settings were held fixed, including the 135-degree continuation threshold. At 0.6 seconds, the first two angles matched five of six available positive track IDs. Both 160 and 155 matched all six, with no extra alerts. The stronger 160-degree threshold is the candidate for held-out validation. It recovered track 154 at 41.23 seconds. Longer persistence matched only two or three available positives. The handoff reports a seventh case whose identity is missing from the inspected label file. These are development results from saved tracks, and the candidate has not been tested on current model inference or independent footage.

Use the clip to check zone placement and prototype speed measurement. Density settings need manual zone counts. A peer-relative speed threshold needs calibrated traversal measurements or reference timing and enough comparable vehicles. Those thresholds remain provisional.
