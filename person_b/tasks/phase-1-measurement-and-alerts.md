# Phase 1 — Measure density and flag unusual trajectories

Use one fixed camera and replay recorded footage through the existing Person B dashboard. Use pilot camera to check integration and compare its existing wrong-way rule. Use rush-hour footage from a Vietnamese junction to evaluate the intended setting. Start with one visible inbound approach and the junction center for density. Restrict both alert rules to a clear, calibrated approach first.

## Tasks

- [ ] **1. Set up the camera and label a small pilot (A + B).** Draw nonoverlapping ground-zone polygons, mark permitted approach direction, and select a straight speed-measurement stretch. Measure ground area and distances if available. Save the geometry against the camera and source resolution. Select complete clips with queues, legal turns, and ordinary traffic. Mark the frames with severe occlusion and keep a separate recording for final checks. **Verify:** Overlay the geometry and confirm that stop lines, direction arrows, and reference distances align. Depends on intersection footage. pilot camera integration can start immediately.

- [ ] **2. Reuse one tracker stream (A).** Feed each bbox, track ID, class, source frame number, and elapsed time from TrafficPipeline to all three measurements. Use the bounding box bottom-center as an approximate ground contact point. Keep identities scoped to a recording and reset motion estimates after gaps or implausible jumps. Log missing observations separately from valid zero counts. **Verify:** Trace exported records to frames, check clock accuracy with stride enabled, and review representative occlusions. Depends on 1 for intersection validation.

- [ ] **3. Measure density by zone (B).** Count each active ID once at each sampled instant. Produce per-class and total counts, then average them over a short window. Report vehicles per 100 square metres only when the ground area is surveyed. Otherwise report vehicles present per zone. Keep any stopped-vehicle count separate. **Verify:** Compare against independent frame annotations in light and dense traffic, with MAE and signed bias per zone. Confirm empty, missing, and polygon-boundary cases. Depends on 1–2.

- [ ] **4. Adapt wrong-way detection (A).** Reuse trajectory smoothing and persistence from the pilot camera rule. Confirm sustained motion in opposition to the permitted approach direction after a minimum displacement. Exclude legal turn regions and stop scoring across track gaps. Emit one candidate per episode with zone, track ID, trigger time, and evidence clip. **Verify:** Match alerts to independently labeled events and review false positives from turns and ID switches. Report precision, recall, false alerts/hour and delay. Record metrics with unavailable denominators as unavailable. Depends on 1–2.

- [ ] **5. Evaluate peer-relative speed (A).** Estimate one representative speed per vehicle over the calibrated stretch using road-plane displacement and elapsed time. Compare it to prior distinct vehicles traveling through the same stretch in the same direction and making a comparable movement. Start with fast outliers. Compare a mean-based baseline with median and MAD on the same records. Require enough comparable peers, a sustained deviation and a minimum speed difference. Record baseline, sample size and comparison score in event evidence. **Verify:** Cross-check estimated speeds against independently timed traversal over a measured distance. Review false alerts on turns, green starts and occlusions. If scale is unavailable, use traversal time over the identical stretch as a relative proxy and omit km/h. Depends on 1–2 and suitable footage.

- [ ] **6. Integrate and evaluate a full replay (A + B).** Add a zone-count chart and two alert types to the existing handoff view. Use one tracking pass. Annotate alerts as candidates and retain the frame/time reference for review. Freeze calibration and thresholds before the held-out replay. **Verify:** Reconcile chart samples and alerts against saved logs, report throughput and alert delay, and document where occlusion prevents reliable measurement. Depends on 3–5.

## Checkpoints

- After 1–2, confirm that the view and time base support the measurements before tuning thresholds.
- After 3–5, review three examples end to end: a dense queue, a wrong-way vehicle, and an unusually fast vehicle. Record explicitly when footage contains too few confirmed examples to evaluate a rule.
- After 6, save the reproducible configuration, metrics and example clips. This phase produces measured density history for later forecast evaluation.
